######## 0.13.2 (pyr-upload-0.13.2)
Pyr 0.13.2

Reel Row Remastered:
- Every one of Reel Row's twelve machines now has its own feature, the way the big slots on Stake have theirs:
  - Tumbles and multiplier bombs (Cosmic Jackpot, Sugar Rush, Neon Nights, Moonlit Cauldron): a win's symbols burst and new ones fall in, again and again while there are wins. Bombs of 2× to 100× (Cosmic Jackpot's to 500×) add up when the tumbling's over and multiply the whole chain.
  - Hold & Win (Deep Sea Treasure, Gold Rush, Tomb of Ra, Dragon's Hoard): coins land with their worth on them, and a collector on the last reel pays them all. Six coins or more hold for three respins, every new coin sending the count back to three, a collector gathering everything held. Mini, Minor and Major coins, and the Grand for filling all fifteen places (Gold Rush's Mother Lode and the Dragon's Hoard: their progressive jackpots).
  - Wilds that grow, freeze, walk and stay: Scoville's Hot Wilds fill their reel with a ×2, ×3 or ×5; Frostbite's freeze while the rest spins again; Starlight Cabaret's step left across the stage; The Daily Grind's Espresso Shots fill their reel and stay a spin.
- Ante, beside the bet: a quarter more a spin, and the bonus about 1.7 times as likely (1.5 on the tumbling machines).
- Buy each machine's feature with Play Feature: Hold & Win straight away, a Bomb Spin, a Hot Spin, a Frozen Spin, an Encore Spin, a Double Shot.
- The last reels slow down and light up when the bonus (or Hold & Win) is a symbol away.
- Each machine's bonus and Hold & Win open with a full-screen splash of their own, and big wins land on new plaques: bronze, silver wings, crystal and lightning, fire, and the crown.
- Every machine has its own band of sounds, made in Pyr: a cantina's guitar and trumpet, a marimba under the sea, synths in space, a music box, a banjo and a saloon piano, synthwave, an oud and a darbuka, a celesta and glass, a harp and an organ, a koto and a gong, a Rhodes and an upright bass, a big band. Pyr Sevens and Ember Dragon get new slot sounds too.
- Every way to play every machine still pays back 99%, checked by playing each of them twenty million times. Every round is still checked by your own Pyr.

Pyr Casino:
- Blackjack, baccarat and keno take up to 100,000 a round, with a 100K chip on the rack.
- The poker table grows with the window: bigger avatars, cards and buttons.
- The casino floor is in chapters (Slots, Poker, Table games, Pyr Originals) with a bar to jump to each, so poker is easy to find.
- Chat is at the table games only.
- Playing craps no longer makes Pyr stop responding.
- Big wins count up through Big, Mega, Epic and Legendary, each level landing with a hit and a burst of coins. A click jumps to the total.
- Turbo on the slots is fast: the reels land almost at once.
- The "funny" tags are gone from the slots.
- "How?" on the floor opens how the casino's fairness works.

Sounds:
- Sound profiles (Settings → Notifications → Sound profile): Pyr's own sounds, or Classic, the simple chimes Pyr started with.
- Ten new ringtones, made in Pyr: Sunrise, Kalimba, Music box, Island, Arcade, Night drive, Lo-fi, Lounge, Old phone and Daydream.
- The built-in soundboard is redrawn, made in Pyr.

Voice and streams:
- Voice isolation takes your PC's own sound out of your microphone (Windows 10 build 20348 or newer, or 11): a match or a game playing from your speakers no longer comes through to everyone while you talk.
- Streamer mode is only ever on when you turn it on.
- A headset that drops out and comes back is no longer reported as an error.

Apps before 0.13.2 keep playing Reel Row as it was; Reel Row Remastered and the 100,000 bets need a 0.13.2 server.

######## 0.13.4 (pyr-upload-0.13.4)
Pyr 0.13.4

Chat:

- The floor's tiles name each game in the middle, the studio centered under it, like Stake's.

- Daily Grind's Cup Shuffle: the background no longer has cups of its own.
- Flash Pot is now Bomb Pot, with new art.

Poker tables:
- New table: a picture of the table instead of a slider. Click a seat to put a bot there and pick which one: how good, and who (each of the 20 bots plays its own way). At a table, clicking an empty seat asks the same.
- Close your own cash tables from the lobby (anyone sitting gets their chips back).
- The buttons before your turn are bigger and say what they'll do: Check / Fold, Check and Call any before anyone bets; Fold, Call 40 and Call any once someone has (a raise takes the tick off Call 40).
- Game Masters wear a winged gold shield over their picture at the table, the Owner of the Game a crowned one, Ambassadors a blue one.

- Winning a hand is slower: the pot stays in the middle a moment so the winning hand can be seen, then slides over; the hand's name stays up longer; an all-in's board comes a street at a time more slowly, and there's longer after a showdown before the next hand.
- Fixed: out of a tournament (or Bomb Pot) and watching, the table said "Shuffle check failed". Your Pyr sent its part for a shuffle it wasn't in any more (the all-in board before was still coming when you went out); nothing was wrong with the shuffle.
- New backgrounds for seven mini games (Meshy's), the place only, with the pieces you play drawn on them: the Champagne Tower's stage has an empty podium in the spotlight, and the tower now stands in the middle of it; the Potion Brew, Treasure Match, the Tomb, the Mine Shaft, Deep Sea and Neon Drop lose the jars, chests, sarcophagi, rocks, clams and pegs painted in behind the real ones.

- Keno: lower your bet: ½ and −(chip) beside +(chip) and 2× (Dice gets −(chip) too).
- The AFK channel, like Discord's: someone in a voice channel whose Pyr has been idle a while (half an hour, until the owner picks 5, 15, 60 or 120 minutes in Server settings) is moved to it. A server with a voice channel called AFK uses it without being told.
- Keno and Dice playing by themselves keep going through wins (a win's show stopped them, even with "A big win stops it" off).
- Game Masters give as many chips as they like (themselves too, and in prize boxes), and they and the Owner of the Game can take chips away. Every gift and every chip taken goes in the staff log, with the balance before and after.
- Reel Row and Ember Dragon: bets up to 100,000 a spin (20,000, 50,000 and 100,000 added). A progressive jackpot pays its whole pool from 10,000 up, as before.
- The Owner of the Game can download the whole staff log (every gift, chip taken and prize box) as a spreadsheet file, saved in Downloads.

Games and plugins with art:
- Torch & Conquer is in the Pyr Library (Plugins): a war of territories on six painted worlds, where the wind fans your attacks and beacons guard your lands. Play bots, friends on one PC, or host a war and send the invite to a channel.

Invite people along:
- Invite someone to your voice channel: right-click their name (or open their card) while you're in one, and pick Invite to the channel. They get a card in the corner of their Pyr, wherever they are in it, with Join.
- Invite someone to your table: the Invite button at the top of any poker, blackjack, roulette, baccarat or craps table. Join takes them straight there.

Bonus rounds that build (from Play Feature):
- Bomb Spins, on the tumbling machines (Cosmic Jackpot, Sugar Rush, Neon Nights, Moonlit Cauldron): ten free spins on richer reels with more bombs. Every bomb on a win adds to a multiplier that stays for the rest of the spins, so each win after is worth more. Three scatters give you five spins more.
- Super Hold & Win, on the Hold & Win machines (Deep Sea Treasure, Gold Rush, Tomb of Ra, Dragon's Hoard): Payers add their worth to every coin on the reels, and Hoarders gather everything again on every respin that brings something new.
- Each one is priced to pay back 99%, like everything else.

New players start with 100,000 chips (20,000 before).

Bounty Poker (new, under Sit & go): six players, 1,500 chips each, a turbo. Half of every buy-in is the price on that player's head, shown on their seat. Knock someone out and half their bounty is paid to you on the spot (BOUNTY +X), the other half goes onto your own head. The top two share the prize pool, and the last one standing collects their own bounty too. 1,000, 10,000, 50,000 or 100,000 to play; bots take the seats nobody came for.

Bomb Pots for 50,000 and 100,000 chips, beside 100, 1,000 and 10,000 (win up to 100 million).

The casino floor:
- The slots' tiles look like Stake's: each name big and white in its own lettering, the studio tiny underneath. What a game pays back shows when you point at it, and your best win sits by how many are playing.
- No more bar down the left: a menu bar across the top instead: the lobby, and Games, which drops down a menu of every game in the casino (the slots, poker, the table games and Pyr Originals, each with its picture and who's playing), then the VIP Club, the leaderboard, your profile, staff and the control room, and Fairness at the end.

The bonus games, clearer:
- Cargo Run: the rocket flies the route drawn in the sky, toward the black hole, with its marks along the way (10×, 50×, 100×, 250×, 720×): the further it gets before its fuel runs out, the more the cargo pays. It says so before you launch.
- Candy Grab: one claw (the machine's own, bigger), riding the rail and reaching into the candy pile where it's going.
- Ice Fishing: one place to fish, the pond the rod's line is in.
- Neon Drop: the pegs sit centred over the bins, and the scene is symmetrical.
- The Heat Ladder: one ladder.
- Ember Respins: the respins left are big and plain, with "a new fireball puts it back to 3", and RESET TO 3! when one does.
- Casts and goes left show big, as lamps.

Slots:
- Total bet up to 100,000 a spin on the slot machines.
- Pyr Sevens' Phoenix Jackpot grows with every spin and pays in proportion to your bet: at the top bet you win all of it, at a tenth of that a tenth. It starts at 10,000,000, and again after it's won. When five flames on your line pay more than your share, you get that and the jackpot keeps growing.

Poker:
- New table sounds, made from scratch: chips with real ring, cards that slide and snap, the felt, the rail, and a better win.
- Game Masters show GAME MASTER on their seat, the Owner too. Any of the staff can hide their title at the tables (Title: shown / hidden, at the top of the table).
- The Genius bots wear a violet star instead of the crown.
- Rabbit hunting: the cards come one at a time, slower, and the next hand waits so you can see them.
- In a smaller window the whole table scales down together, the Fold, Call and Raise buttons too (they used to stay big over a small table).
- Your hand and its chance of winning never sit on another player: they move under your cards' other end, under your seat or to your other side when someone's there.

- Blackjack: the Crown Room. Baccarat: the Imperial Salon (Tie and the pairs up to the table's most too). Craps: the Sky Rollers (full odds behind, up to 500,000,000 on the layout). Roulette: the Grand Salle (500,000 on a number, 10,000,000 on red or black, and the rest in between).
- New chips for them: 500K, 1M and 5M.

High stakes at the tables:
- Craps: the High Rollers table takes 500 to 100,000 a bet, with full odds behind the pass line and up to 2,500,000 on the layout at once. New: lay odds behind don't pass (up to 6 times it, paid at true odds on a seven), for the dark side.

- Roulette: the Riviera High Rollers take up to each spot's own most: 25,000 on a number, 500,000 on red or black, even or odd, high or low.

Dice: rolling by itself, a win stays up a moment to be seen (a big one longer) before the next roll.

######## 0.13.26 (pyr-upload-0.13.26)

- Screen sharing, done the way Discord does it: the picture stays on the graphics card all the way out. Windows hands each frame over on the card, the card's video engine makes it the size it goes out at, and the card's own encoder (NVIDIA's NVENC, or AMD's or Intel's) encodes it; the processor only sends what comes out. Before, every frame came back from the card to the processor, which scaled, converted and encoded it (OpenH264), and a game taking the processor left a 1080p share a frame or two a second. In a test on a PC with a 4070 Ti and a 4K screen, the old way took 26.5 ms a frame and reached 32 frames a second with nothing else running; on the card it took 3.4 ms and kept a steady 60. - The call's stats say what's encoding your share (NVIDIA encoder, AMD encoder, Intel encoder, or processor encoder) and how many frames a second really go out.
