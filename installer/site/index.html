Questions

Good questions, straight answers

Is Pyr really free?

Yes. The app is free and Pyr never charges you. The only cost is your group's server, if you rent one: about $4–6 a month from DigitalOcean (or whichever host you pick), billed by them, for the whole group. Hosting it on your own PC is free; the PC just has to stay on.

Does everyone need a server?

No. One person sets up the server, and everyone else installs Pyr and opens the invite link. You can be in as many servers as you like.

Windows says "Windows protected your PC". Is Pyr safe?

Windows shows that for new programs that aren't code-signed yet, and Pyr is new. Click More info, then Run anyway. You don't need to turn anything off.

Want to check first? The code is on GitHub, and the download's SHA-256 is shown after you click Download. After that, updates are checked against Firelit Games' signature.

Who can see our messages?

Only the people who can read where they were written. Messages and pictures in every channel, direct message and group are end-to-end encrypted, and so are calls (voice, cameras, screens and stream sound). The server keeps only scrambled text it can't read. It still knows who wrote when and where, and who can read where, since it decides who's in.

Can I use Pyr on a Mac or my phone?

Not yet. Pyr runs on Windows 10 and 11, and on Linux (Ubuntu 22.04 or newer, Linux Mint 21+, Pop!_OS, Debian 12+).

Can we move our server later?

Yes. The owner's Pyr backs the server up to their PC every hour, and a backup sets up the same server on any other host, with all its history. You're never tied to one provider.

How do updates work?

Pyr checks for a new version every few hours (or when you click its version number at the top of the window). When there is one, a green Update button shows up there: click it, and Pyr downloads it, checks Firelit Games' signature, installs it and puts you back in your voice channel.

How do I uninstall it?

On Windows, use Settings → Apps → Pyr → Uninstall. Your identity and settings stay in %APPDATA%\Pyr, in case you come back. On Linux, run sudo apt remove pyr.
